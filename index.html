Sizes a pressure vessel's wall from ASME Section VIII-1 (UG-27, allowable stress from ASME II-D 2013), back-calculates MAWP and MAWT, screens materials of construction for the service (corrosion allowance vs. cracking mechanisms), and builds purchase + itemized installation cost (Seider Ch. 16, Guthrie line items) across a diameter sweep. A second tab applies the same engine to the Haber-Bosch synthesis-loop pressure trade-off: equilibrium conversion and recycle vs. converter/separator wall and compression.

    Calculation chain

    1. Design conditions (Seider §16.5): Pd = 10 psig if Po ≤ 5 psig; exp{0.60608 + 0.91615 ln Po + 0.0015655 (ln Po)²} for 5–1000 psig; 1.1·Po above 1000 psig. Td = To + 50 °F.
    2. Allowable stress S(Td): ASME II-D 2013 Table 1A/1B, Section VIII-1 column, linear interpolation. Local file asme_iid_stress.json (page refs per material). Blank above the material's VIII-1 temperature limit.
    3. Pressure thickness: UG-27 tp = PdR/(SE − 0.6Pd) (= Seider 16.60); Appendix 1-2 thick-wall form t = R(eP/SE − 1) when P > 0.385SE. E = 0.85 (spot RT) up to 1.25 in, 1.0 above (Seider).
    4. Vertical wind/seismic (Seider 16.62): tw = 0.22(Do+18)L²/(S Do²); tv = tp + tw/2 (average of top and bottom).
    5. Minimum thickness (Seider): ¼ in to 4 ft, 5/16 to 6 ft, 3/8 to 8 ft, 7/16 to 10 ft, ½ in to 12 ft; +1/16 in per 2 ft above 12 ft (extrapolated).
    6. Corrosion allowance = rate × design life (min 1/16 in). Credit toggle: off → t = max(tp, tmin) + CA (Seider); on → t = max(tp + CA, tmin) (Coulson, minimum includes CA). Round up to plate: 1/16 in steps to ½ in, 1/8 in to 2 in, ¼ in above.
    7. MAWP: UG-27 inverted on the corroded thickness (tnom − CA) at Td. MAWT: highest temperature at which S(T) still gives MAWP ≥ Pd on the corroded wall, capped at the VIII-1 limit. Corrosion life = (tnom − tp)/rate: all metal above the pressure requirement, including min-thickness and plate-rounding surplus.
    8. Weight (Seider 16.59): W = π(Di+t)(L+0.8Di)t·ρ with ρ = 0.284 lb/in³ (Seider's cost basis; heads same thickness as shell). Actual shipping mass uses the material's own density.
    9. Purchase cost (Seider 16.52–16.56, CE 567): CP = FMCV + CPL. Vertical CV = exp{7.1390 + 0.18255 lnW + 0.02297(lnW)²}, CPL = 410D0.7396L0.70684; horizontal CV = exp{5.6336 + 0.4599 lnW + 0.00582(lnW)²}, CPL = 2275D0.2094. FM from Seider Table 16.26.
    10. Installation, itemized: Guthrie line items (Seider Table 16.10: piping 0.456, concrete 0.051, steel 0.031, instruments 0.102, electrical 0.020, insulation 0.049, paint 0.005, erection 0.554, setting 0.076, freight 0.080, overhead 0.571, engineering 0.296 × CP; sum 2.291) scaled so they total the vessel bare-module factor FBM − 1 (Seider Table 16.11: vertical 4.16, horizontal 3.05). Installation is charged on the carbon-steel purchase cost (Seider Eq. 16.12). Assumption: vessel line items share the heat-exchanger split.
    11. Contingency + fee: 15% + 3% (Seider, on direct permanent investment ≈ bare-module cost for one item).
    12. Cost year: escalated CE 567 → 800 (single fixed index; relative comparisons are the point of this tool).

    Service screening

    Follows the Plant Resources\Materials of Construction methodology (per-mechanism, UNS-named, Perry's 9th Table 25-3 ratings, FEL-1 screening only). Corrosion rates are illustrative placeholders pending a sourced corrosion database (NACE/MTI data are not on disk). Hot-hydrogen limits are an approximate reading of API 941 Nelson curves at high H₂ partial pressure. Verify before use.

    Haber-Bosch case study

    Equilibrium: Gillespie-Beattie Kp (atm⁻¹, ½N₂ + 3/2H₂ ⇌ NH₃), ideal gas (fugacity corrections neglected, which understates conversion at high P), 3:1 H₂:N₂ plus inerts. Per-pass outlet NH₃ = inlet + approach × (equilibrium − inlet). Inlet NH₃ = NH₃ vapour pressure at the separator T ÷ loop P. Loop flow F = ξ(1+yout)/(yout − yin). Converter catalyst volume = converter inlet flow at STP ÷ GHSV (standard-condition space velocity, as industrial converters are rated); vessel volume = 1.3 × catalyst. Because the catalyst volume does not shrink with pressure, wall metal (∝ P × V) and converter capital rise with loop pressure. A residence time at actual conditions would make V ∝ 1/P and cancel that effect exactly (pV = nRT). Separator: Souders-Brown K = 0.107 m/s (Ammonia Process knock-out basis), L/D 3.5. Makeup compressor: intercooled (40 °C), equal ratios ≤ 3 per stage, ideal-gas polytropic; circulator lifts loop flow by the loop ΔP. Compressor cost: Seider Eq. 16.36 centrifugal CB = exp{9.1553 + 0.63 ln PC} (hp, CE 567, includes motor) × FD (1.15 for a steam-turbine drive, Eq. 16.35), installed ×2.15. Compression energy: electric drive = grid price ÷ 0.95 motor efficiency; steam-turbine drive = steam rate × blended steam value, where the blend is the share of turbine steam raised from recovered reformer convection-section / loop WHB heat (default $0.53/t, its boiler-feed-water cost, i.e. surplus heat with no other use) vs make-up steam that must be fired or bought (default $17.60/t, Seider Table 17.1, 450 psig). Default steam rate 3.45 kg/kWh: condensing turbine, 105 bar / 510 °C (Ammonia Process Memoir HP header) to 0.10 bar, 80% isentropic (IAPWS-95); a back-pressure turbine to 4.5 bar needs ≈ 5.9 kg/kWh. Integration only pays when the steam is genuinely surplus: if it could otherwise run a turbo-generator, its value approaches the grid price and the steam drive saves little. The recovered-steam supply is not capped (no plant steam balance). Refrigeration duty is not included. Proof of concept: trends, not a design.
